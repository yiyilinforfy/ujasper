UNIVERSITY OF JASPER

THE JASPER FIELD SEMESTER

Jasper, Alberta, Canada | English curriculum edition: 24 September 2026



VISION

To establish in Jasper a university for the world, where people across cultures and backgrounds develop physical strength, depth of knowledge, independent minds and integrity. We envision a lifelong community that defends freedom, pursues truth, creates with courage and accepts public responsibility, whose members advance human flourishing through their own growth, their example and their contribution to others.



MISSION

To unite sustained physical training, humanistic learning across world civilizations, and original inquiry in mathematics, physics, chemistry and economics. Through life in nature, lectures, close dialogue, debate, field study, experiments and shared projects, we cultivate people of courage, deep learning, independent judgment, initiative, self-discipline and a commitment to justice. We use AI to extend inquiry while strengthening human understanding, creativity and responsibility.



FORMAT

Six weeks in person; 6-8 adults aged 18+; four teaching days weekly; 144 facilitated hours; three proposed camp nights. Personal phones/networked devices off during learning and camps; no internet or live AI in the core. Private medical/access/essential-contact exceptions and staff emergency communications remain available.



COURSES

JAS 101 | Fieldcraft, Hiking & Camping | 36 hours | A route-and-camp dossier; observed field judgment.

JAS 102 | Martial Arts & Restraint | 18 hours | A coached control demonstration; practice record.

JAS 103 | Attention & Reflective Practice | 12 hours | An attention-method comparison; personal protocol.

JAS 104 | Mathematics & Physics in Nature | 30 hours | A reproducible investigation; individual oral defence.

JAS 105 | Philosophy, Reading & Argument | 30 hours | Text annotations, a fair opposing argument, revised essay.

JAS 106 | The Commons & Original Inquiry | 18 hours | A useful analogue artifact; stewardship and cost account.



WEEKLY ARC

1. Attention: What do I notice, and what do I merely assume? Evidence: Observation baseline; map-and-measure sheet; 250-word position.

2. Discipline: When does restraint make us stronger? Evidence: First camp review; balanced practice plan; opportunity-cost account.

3. Evidence: What would change my mind? Evidence: Repeatable experiment; uncertainty record; revised claim.

4. Freedom: What makes disagreement productive? Evidence: Structured debate; printed-claim audit; capstone proposal.

5. Responsibility: What do I owe to a place and to other people? Evidence: Two-night camp dossier; prototype test; stewardship evidence.

6. Contribution: What can I now explain, do and give? Evidence: Final portfolio; individual oral defence; 30-day commitment.



ACADEMIC CALENDAR

Winter break: mid-December to mid-February, approximately two months. No required teaching or camping takes place during this period. Six-week cohorts finish before the break or begin after it. Exact dates are confirmed each academic year.

TIMETABLE

09:00-16:15 each day: 360 teaching minutes plus 75 minutes for meals/breaks. Day A: attention 15m, science 150m, martial arts 90m, philosophy 105m. Day B: attention 15m, fieldcraft 180m, philosophy 90m, commons 75m. Day C: attention 15m, science 150m, martial arts 90m, attention seminar 60m, commons 45m. Day D: attention 15m, fieldcraft 180m, philosophy 105m, commons 60m.

Camp weeks: A Tuesday, C Wednesday, B Thursday, D Friday. Week 2: Thursday night. Week 5: Thursday and Friday nights; Saturday checkout/return. Camp meals, sleep, transport and duty are outside the 144 teaching hours.



ASSESSMENT

Six weekly entries, capstone dossier, individual oral defence, instructor-reviewed field and martial demonstrations. Five dimensions: practice/care; evidence/reasoning; interpretation/argument; original work/revision; contribution/autonomy. Revision opportunities and equivalent adapted tasks. Descriptive non-credit participation record only.
