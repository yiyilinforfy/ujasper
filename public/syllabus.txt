UNIVERSITY OF JASPER
FIELD SEMESTER SYLLABUS
Edition: 15 October 2026

University of Jasper is a founding project in Jasper, Alberta, Canada. Its first proposed program is non-degree. No degree-granting authority or current institutional approval is claimed. A venue, teaching appointments, funding and launch dates remain unconfirmed.

6 weeks in person in Jasper; 6–8 adults aged 18+. 144 facilitated hours + 24 independent-study hours = 168 academic hours. Three proposed camp nights, one in Week 2 and two in Week 5.
Winter break: mid-December to mid-February. Optional AI Return Lab: 3 additional hours after the offline semester, separately arranged.

COURSES
Fieldcraft and physical practice | 36 facilitated hours
Martial arts and restraint | 18 facilitated hours
Attention and reflective practice | 12 facilitated hours
Jasper as Laboratory | 30 facilitated hours
Philosophy and the Jasper Tutorial | 30 facilitated hours
The Commons and original inquiry | 18 facilitated hours

WEEKLY ARC
1 · Attention
What do I notice, and what do I merely assume?
Read: Plato, Apology: selected passages on examination and responsibility; Laozi, Tao Te Ching: Chapters 1, 2 and 11. Compare what argument reveals with what naming may conceal.
Evidence: A 400–600-word opening argument, two annotated passages and a tutorial revision.

2 · Discipline
When does restraint make us stronger?
Read: Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.1–6; Confucius, Analects 2.4, 4.17, 12.1 and 15.24. Read Dhammapada II, verses 21–32 as a short third perspective on attention and conduct.
Evidence: An Essay 1 draft connecting a precise textual claim with one observed practice; distinguish habit from conformity.

3 · Evidence
What would change my mind?
Read: Wang Yangming’s knowledge/action exchange recorded by Xu Ai, paired with Hume, Enquiry IV on experience and inference. Preserve their different questions and historical settings.
Evidence: Essay 1 revised to 1,200–1,500 words or equivalent; answer a counterexample in tutorial.

4 · Freedom
What makes disagreement productive?
Read: Mill, On Liberty II; Wollstonecraft, Vindication II. Examine the institutions and educational opportunities needed for meaningful independence.
Evidence: A 600–800-word Essay 2 proposal, an opposing argument represented fairly and a learner-council decision record.

5 · Responsibility
What do I owe to a place and to other people?
Read: Selected passages from Ostrom, Governing the Commons, Chapter 3, alongside Tagore, Gitanjali 35–36 and local published mapping history. Test an institutional rule against a concrete shared-resource problem.
Evidence: Essay 2 draft; a proposed rule with monitoring, revision and a fair account of who bears its costs.

6 · Contribution
What can I now explain, do and give?
Read: Douglass, Narrative VI–VII; Epictetus, Enchiridion 1, 5 and 30. Ask what requires personal practice and what requires changing institutions.
Evidence: Essay 2 revised to 1,200–1,500 words or equivalent; individual defence and a justified continuation plan.

PARTICIPATION
Personal phones, internet and live AI are off during teaching and camps, with agreed private medical, accessibility and essential-contact exceptions. Staff emergency communications remain active. Storage is voluntary and devices are returned on request.

ASSESSMENT
Two revised essays 1,200–1,500 words each; scientific dossier 800–1,200 words or agreed equivalents; six weekly entries; useful original work; coached demonstrations and individual defence. Five dimensions, levels 0–3; reach level 2 after revision.

General inquiries are welcome at vincent@ujasper.ca. Formal admissions and appointments are not open. Website forms prepare local drafts and never submit automatically; a draft is not an application, reservation or offer.
